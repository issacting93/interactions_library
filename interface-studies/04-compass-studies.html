Compass Studies / 4 directions

One heading, four instruments. Drag any compass to turn it, or focus one and use the arrow keys. Each direction borrows from a different part of the sci-fi interface lineage.

HDG 000

Reliquary Instrument I

Ash Thorp · Lost Boy, Project 2501

Monochrome hardware with one red index. Heavy bezel, condensed numerals, film grain. The card has mass: it overshoots and settles like a damped needle, so it feels like an object, not a screen.

Suits the physical pieces, such as a screen embedded in The Witness's plinth.

Datamatic Tape II

Ryoji Ikeda · data.tron, test pattern

No dial at all. Heading is a scrolling tape, and the barcode band is generated from the heading itself, so turning writes data. Pure black and white, numbers as material, no easing: the readout is the raw value.

Suits the surveillance and logging layer: the side of the system that counts.

Heaven Plate III

Luopan · NERV terminals · Tsutomu Nihei

A working luopan behind a warning-orange terminal. Later Heaven trigrams, the 24 mountains, red cross threads and a live sitting/facing reading (坐/向). It reads exactly as a geomancer would read the real instrument.

Suits the Chinese cosmology thread of Cyber Reliquary.

Volumetric Bearing IV

GMUNK · Territory Studio · Jayse Hansen

A stack of light rings seen in perspective, with the current heading at the front. Every readout is real: magnetic to true heading using New York's declination, and the great-circle bearing and distance to a waypoint.

Suits an exhibition screen, where plausibility sells the fiction.

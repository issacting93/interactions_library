Cybernetic Ecology — field notes

Species Codex

Seventeen animals live in the tank. Nine are fully original now — six were hand-drawn from the start, and three more (Bell Jelly, Blade Puffer, Winged Dragon) started as recombinations of a shared 71-part library and were later redrawn whole, so they no longer borrow anything. The remaining eight are still true remixes of that original library. This page catalogs all seventeen by anatomy, morphology, lineage, and behavior, then asks what an eighteenth could be made of.

01 · Anatomy

Eleven slots, seventy-one parts

Every piece of every animal — hand-drawn or remixed — fills exactly one of eleven anatomical slots. A part can only ever swap with another part in its own slot: a tail for a tail, a wing for a wing. That constraint is what makes the remix species possible — the rig doesn't care whose sigil a part came from, only which slot it fills.

02 · Morphology

One body plan, used four times

Measured by real bounding-box height over width — not a guess. Four of the seventeen are dramatically taller than they are wide: a chain of body plates strung down a long spine, the oldest and most reused shape in the library. They read as "the worm ones" because, structurally, they nearly are.

03 · Lineage

Nine originals, eight remixes

The nine root sigils are the only animals with a part marked "own" in the library — every other part they use, they drew themselves. Six were hand-drawn from the start; three more (Bell Jelly, Blade Puffer, Winged Dragon) began as remixes and were later redrawn whole, so nothing on them is borrowed anymore. The eight derived species still own nothing: every part on their body is on loan, traced back to a root's slot. Five of the eight were given their own reference drawing in sigil-species-rig-svgs anyway. The other three had no standalone file there, but Metamorphosis.html — a separate tool for watching one creature transform into the next — already carried their real part geometry; each one below marked "From Metamorphosis" was extracted straight out of it, not redrawn.

04 · Growth chain

Eat enough, change species

Seven of the seventeen sit on a single food chain, not a size slider: a wisp that eats enough becomes a swift, a swift that eats enough becomes a koi, and a koi forks into a manta or a hunter. The two ends of the fork — eel and rogue — are apex stage: they never evolve again, and instead die of old age and sink, feeding whatever grazes the bottom (wisp, pincer).

06 · Proposed

An eighteenth, already underway

Not yet in the simulation — no slot composition, no behavior table, no population — but real art, drawn front-on rather than as a lateral swim profile. A second take on the puffed-up silhouette already seen on Blade Puffer, this time with fin-spikes instead of crystal blades.

07 · An eighteenth species

What else the library could make

Nothing here is hypothetical — every mechanism below is already running somewhere in the other seventeen. A new species mostly means deciding which of these five levers to pull, not drawing from scratch.

01

Pure remix

Pick a slot-combination nobody's tried and assign it zero new art — exactly how eight of the seventeen already exist. The limit isn't drawing time, it's finding an unused combination that still reads as one animal.

02

Partial draw

Draw one new part for one slot — a tail, say — and borrow the other ten slots from the library. Cheapest way to get a silhouette nothing else has, for the cost of a single piece.

03

New slot

Introduce a slot the library doesn't have yet — a dorsal spike, a gill frill, a bioluminescent core. It needs fresh art, but the rig and the shader already animate any number of slots generically; a twelfth slot is free to move once it's drawn.

04

Behavioral mutation

Change nothing about the art — just the swim and mood numbers (amplitude, lag, turn rate, school weight). Same body, new personality. Several of the seventeen already differ from a close relative mostly in these dials, not in their parts.

05

Extend the chain

Add a new link or a third fork to the growth chain — a stage between hunter and rogue, or a second branch off manta. The chain is just a lookup table of "eat N, become Y"; it has room for more stages than it currently uses.

Three shapes the tank doesn't have yet

Grounded in the Morphology section above — these target the actual gaps in the aspect-ratio spread, not a mood board.

A

A short stub

Same plate-chain vocabulary as Serpent or Eel — just two or three plates instead of ten, stopped early. Proves the worm-like silhouette was an authoring choice, not a limit of the parts themselves.

B

A forked spine

Every animal here is one spine, start to end. Nothing branches. A creature with two tails off one head — physically the shape the growth chain already draws as a diagram — has never been built as a body.

C

A flatter disc

Puffer and Jelly already broke from the long-spine default, but neither goes very wide. Something closer to a true ray — almost no body length, mostly rib and ornament — would sit past both of them on the aspect-ratio chart.

Two kinds of unexplored

Among the original six, every pair has been crossed at least once — except one. Hunter and Angler have never been blended. A hunter's flank blades and fangs, fused with an angler's lure and crown, would be a predator-ambusher nobody has built.

And the three newest roots — Jelly, Puffer, and Dragon — have never lent a part to anything. They're full of original, unshared pieces (a wing-pair, a lantern crown, a blade-ring) that no remix has ever touched. That's not a gap in the matrix; it's an entire unused wing of the library.

08 · Eight new species

Eight real animals, run through the same library

Each of these takes one real underwater or microscopic organism — a tardigrade, a mantis shrimp, a radiolarian, a comb jelly, a vampire squid, a giant isopod, a leafy seadragon, a siphonophore — and designs it as a pure remix: zero new art, just an unused slot-combination from the existing 71 parts, same as eight of the current seventeen. Between them they fill every gap called out above: all three morphology holes (a short stub, a forked spine, a flatter disc), the one never-tried root cross (Hunter × Angler), and all three roots that have never lent a part (Jelly, Puffer, Dragon). None of these are drawn or simulated yet — they're composition recipes, in the same format the library already uses to describe the eight remixes that do exist.

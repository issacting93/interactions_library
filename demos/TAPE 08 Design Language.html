A tape machine
drawn in hairlines.

TAPE 08 is an 8-track looper that looks like an instrument panel from a 1970s mission console, rebuilt with today's spacing. Thin off-white strokes on warm charcoal do the structural work, monospaced readouts carry the numbers, and colour appears only when it means something: amber for transport, red for record, green for loop, one hue per track.

01

Principles

Read these first

i

Draw, don't fill

Structure is a 1–1.5 px line on charcoal: reels, rails, rulers, knob tracks. Solid fills are reserved for active state, so a filled shape always means "on".

ii

Colour is a signal

Every colour has a single job. Amber is transport and focus, red is record, green is loop, cyan is solo. Track hues identify audio and nothing else.

iii

Readouts are monospaced

Anything that counts, such as timecode, dB, BPM or bar numbers, is set in a monospace font with tabular figures, so the digits stay put while they change.

iv

Soft to the touch

Corners are rounded so controls feel like physical keys. Radius grows with size, from 3 px ticks to 22 px panels.

v

Depth by step

No drop shadows. Layers separate with one surface step lighter and a hairline border; the selected item steps up once more.

vi

Built for fingers

Every control is at least 44 px. Labels sit beside values, never inside tooltips, so a performer can read the panel at arm's length.

02

Colour

Charcoal · bone · four signals

The neutrals lean warm, toward the amber accent, so the panel reads as aged instrument plastic rather than a cold screen. Surfaces step up in small, even increments. Never place a track hue on a control, and never use a signal colour for decoration.

Track palette · one hue per track, in this order

  • Hues share chroma and lightness so no track shouts. Each one appears as the track's number underline, its waveform stroke, its clip baseline and its FX rail.
  • Muted or un-soloed tracks drop to 32 % opacity as a whole row. The hue is never swapped for grey.
03

Type

Two monospaced faces

Martian Mono is the display face, a wide mono with a mission-console accent, used for anything you read from a distance: timecode, track numbers, effect names and big readouts. IBM Plex Mono handles labels and interface text. Labels are uppercase with wide tracking; values keep their natural case and units.

05

Icons

Stroke · currentColor · 12–18 px

  • Draw icons inline, at 1.2–1.3 px on a 12–18 px box, with square caps. Only transport glyphs (play, pause, stop, record) are filled.
  • Icons inherit currentColor, so state colour comes from the button. Icon-only buttons always carry an aria-label.
  • No emoji and no icon fonts.
06

Components

Live specimens · tap to toggle

6.1 Transport buttons

btn · btn.play · btn.rec
  • 44 px tall, 10 px radius, 11 px caps at .22em. At rest a button is outlined in its signal colour; when active it fills with that colour and its text switches to ground.
  • Rec is the widest button in the cluster. Destructive actions such as Clear use ink-2, never red.

6.2 Track toggles

arm · sqbtn.m · sqbtn.s · led
  • Arm is the only circular toggle: a hollow red ring, filled when armed. Selecting a track arms it.
  • M fills amber and S fills cyan. The LED switch is used for effect bypass, takes the effect's colour, and swaps its label between ON and BYP.

6.3 Knob

270° sweep · unipolar / bipolar
  • A 270° track starts at 7 o'clock and is drawn at 16 % ink. The value arc uses the owner's colour, and the pointer is ink.
  • Bipolar parameters such as EQ, bump and output fill outward from 12 o'clock. The value (Martian 11) sits above the label (Plex 8 caps).
  • Turn by vertical drag or the arrow keys. Double-click resets to default.

6.4 Faders

horizontal · vertical
+60-6-12-24-48-∞
  • The track is a 1 px line at 22 % ink. The horizontal thumb is a 6×16 px bone bar with a 3 px radius.
  • The vertical cap is 30×14 px with a 5 px radius, outlined in ink, with a 2 px tick in the channel's colour. A 0 dB detent mark sits at 18 % of the travel from the top.

  • The timecode always shows minutes:seconds.tenths, with tabular digits and a 7-tick scale beneath it.
  • The status dot blinks once a second only while recording, and stops blinking under reduced motion.

6.7 Ruler, loop bracket & playhead

24 bars · labels every 4
  • Bar ticks are 9 px at 25 % ink, and every fourth bar gets a 14 px tick at 50 % ink with a label.
  • The loop bracket is a 2 px green line with 10 px end dots. The playhead is a 14 px rounded square outlined in amber; its 1 px ink line runs down through every lane.

6.8 Track row

header · lane · controls · 64 px
  • Header: the number in Martian 18 (amber when selected, ink-3 otherwise), the track's colour as a 2 px underline, then the name and input.
  • Lane: 12 px radius with a faint 16-bar grid. Clips are waveform strokes in the track's colour, with a 3 px baseline bar and a micro label. When selected, the lane steps up a surface and gets an amber border.
  • Empty lanes show a dashed centre line and the words "Empty · arm to record". On narrow screens the controls wrap beneath the lane.

6.9 FX card

signal rail · LED · 4-up knobs
  • Cards hang off a dashed signal rail in the effect's colour, with a square node at each card. When the effect is bypassed the rail turns grey, the node goes hollow and the knobs dim to 30 %.
  • Header: a circular index badge, the name in Martian 13, move up and down, the LED switch, and remove. Knobs sit in a 4-column grid.

6.10 Tape deck

reels · tape path · head
  • The supply reel holds a thick tape pack and the take-up reel a thin one. Each reel has three spokes and a hub, and the wound tape is drawn in #2A2A2E.
  • The reels spin once every 2.6 s during playback and every 1.4 s while recording. The amber line marks the record head.

6.11 Tempo stepper

stepper
120BPM
  • A 12 px-radius group: two 40×44 px buttons around a 64 px readout in Martian 17 amber, with a micro unit label below it.

6.12 View tabs

top strip
ArrangeMixEditExport
  • 11 px caps at .22em. The current view is shown in ink with a 1 px amber underline; other views stay ink-2 until hovered.

Mechanical, linear, purposeful

  • No bounces, springs or fades on controls. Motion only shows tape moving.
  • Under prefers-reduced-motion, the reels and blink stop; the playhead and meters keep moving because they carry information.
09

Labels & units

Write like a front panel

Rules

  • Labels are one or two words, uppercase: SELECTED TRACK, TEMPO / SIG, CLIP HOLD.
  • Always show the unit next to the number, at a smaller size in ink-2: −6.2 dB, 250 ms, 4.2k, 48 kHz.
  • Use a true minus (−) and −∞ for silence. Pad track numbers to two digits (01–08) and bars to three (BAR 007.3).
  • Use the middle dot to join related facts (IN 1 · MONO) and the arrow for routing (MBP MIC → MBP SPK).
  • Name actions by their result: REC, STOP, CLEAR, + ADD, never "Submit" or "OK".

10

Do / Don't

Quick review checklist

DO
  • Build structure from 1–1.5 px ink lines; reserve fills for active state.
  • Keep each signal colour to its one meaning.
  • Set every number in a monospace font with tabular figures and its unit.
  • Give every control a 44 px target and a visible focus ring.
  • Separate layers with a surface step plus a hairline.
  • Dim whole muted rows to 32 % opacity.
DON'T
  • Add drop shadows, glows, gradients or glassmorphism.
  • Colour a button with a track hue, or use red for anything except record and clip.
  • Use proportional fonts for readouts, or Inter and Roboto anywhere.
  • Hide values in tooltips or put labels inside inputs.
  • Animate controls with easing, springs or bounces.
  • Use emoji or filled icons, apart from the transport glyphs.

Grammar

Six kinds of rule decide what goes where. The street plan is solved first on the ground; a second pass stacks storeys, roofs and towers on top of it.

1 · Sockets match

Neighbouring pieces must show the same label on the edge they share.

LabelMeaningWalk

2 · Ring zoning

Each ring only accepts pieces from its own district, so the city reads inside-out like the Round City of Baghdad.

RingCellsDistrictPieces

3 · Splitting outward

Where a ring has twice the cells of the one inside it, a piece's outer edge touches two pieces and both must match it.

RINGS = [1, 8, 16, 32, 32, 32, 32, 64, 64]

4 · Fixed positions

ring 8, j ∈ {0, 16, 32, 48}  →  gate only
ring 8, any other j          →  never a gate
cells on a gate's bearing    →  street in and out

5 · Everything reaches the core

A walk from the mosque follows plaza, street and arcade edges. Streets it never reaches are re-solved as closed buildings.

—

6 · Stacking

Every cell is a column of four slots above its ground piece. A module's bottom socket must equal the top socket below it, so towers end in caps and storeys end in a roof, dome, windcatcher or talar.
